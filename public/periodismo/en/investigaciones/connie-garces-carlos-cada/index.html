The bomb and the narco's police officers

A bomb at a TV presenter's shop opened the door to the Los Lagartos organization: Connie Garcés, a model and Guayaquil television personality, was the partner of Pedro Sánchez, the second-in-command of alias Marino. Then came the names behind Marino's murder and the photos of police officers who reported to a drug trafficker.

In four sentences
  1. On March 4, 2026, at 02:10, two men on a motorcycle threw an explosive at New Phone, a cellphone shop in Urdesa, Guayaquil, linked to presenter and model Connie Garcés. The day before, they had shot at the shop. The flyer read: the mafia's doll, tell your husband this isn't extortion, pay up the money.
  2. An unusual source, who wanted to tell things before it was too late, had warned days earlier: Garcés is the partner of Pedro Sánchez, a former police officer and second-in-command to Stalin Olivero, alias Marino, and she was at the Mocolí soccer field on January 7, when he was killed. Sánchez set her up with the shop. Before that, Garcés had been the partner of Cojo Ronald, a Los Lagartos leader, of Samir Maestre, alias La S, and briefly of police officer José Luis Cantuña, all three murdered. In the underworld they call her the widow.
  3. On March 6 we reconstructed, with nearly a dozen sources inside the organization, how Marino was killed: suspicion points to Sánchez himself, for reasons of the heart and of the wallet, using the same team of hitmen that had earlier executed Samir. Micaela Morales, the fiancée, asked that day for a list of Marino's assets and changed her phone number.
  4. On March 13 we showed the photos police officers sent to drug trafficker Carlos Kada to let him know his competitors' drugs had already been seized. Two officers identified, a major and a lieutenant, both on active duty. The report is with the police. The interior minister and the police commander had not acted.
Case statusInvestigation ongoing; the police report is still sitting in a drawer

Pedro Sánchez, identified by sources as the number two of Los Lagartos, is out of the country. Connie Garcés faces no criminal case. Carlos Kada was murdered weeks after the information he gave the Anti-Corruption Unit leaked from that very unit. The report naming the officers who reported to him is with the National Police; as of March 13, 2026, nobody had requested it.

Installment 1

A bomb in Urdesa

The source mentioned Connie Garcés days earlier. The source said she was the partner of Pedro Sánchez, alias Marino's number two, sent documents and told us to watch the news. The name had been in Mónica Velásquez's notebook since January 8, the day after Marino's death, jotted in the margin: Connie and Pedro, second. On March 3 someone shot at New Phone, a cellphone shop on Víctor Emilio Estrada avenue in Urdesa; the employees thought it was an extortion "vaccine." On March 4, 2026, at 02:10, two men on a motorcycle threw an explosive. The shutter was destroyed, the glass door shattered, part of the ceiling on the floor. A flyer with the names blacked out read: the mafia's doll, tell your husband this isn't extortion, pay up the money. The money: about ten million dollars that Los Lagartos claim from Sánchez.

No one was hurt. Minutes after the report appeared on social media, the source wrote: now you believe me.

The attack on Conny Garcés and her ties to Los Lagartos and the drug trade

05/03/2026 · Original on YouTube ↗

We spent a whole day with Mónica Velásquez going over the life of someone whose name we always had and never examined. We spoke with members of Los Lagartos. Garcés, an Ecuavisa actress and presenter, model, 30, engineer, recently back from Argentina, was the partner of Ronald Sánchez León, Cojo Ronald, one of the leaders of Los Lagartos, who was murdered. Then of Samir Maestre, alias La S, the man who replaced Leandro Norero in the organization, murdered at his home in Samborondón by hired police officers, on Marino's orders according to sources. Briefly, of drug-trafficking police officer José Luis Cantuña, murdered a year ago. And then of Pedro Sánchez. According to those who handle Sánchez's money in the gang, he set her up with the shop after a fight. Nobody asks about the Mitsubishi Montero Sport and the previous-year BMW X5 in her name, worth more than 150,000 dollars, or the house in Villaclub in her mother's name, or the Rolex watches, on a television salary.

Is the Garcés girl a narco? No. She doesn't traffic; no source has suggested that. The problem is that the public voice in Ecuador is still in the hands of people who go to bed with organized crime.

Andersson Boscán, March 5, 2026

Installment 2

The number two

Pedro Sánchez is a former police officer, born in 1985, Marino's right-hand man and the number two of Los Lagartos. There are few photos of him: he moves with a guard of ten or twelve men in pickup trucks around his armored car. He was at the Mocolí soccer field, rented by Andrés Vélez Arcos, Micaela Morales's brother, on January 7, 2026, lying face down like everyone else, watching the hitmen's boots go by as they checked faces one by one, when the armed group killed Marino and two of his bodyguards. He survived. Today, according to our sources, he is out of the country: he tried to take control of the organization and ran into an internal rebellion. That same January 7, the organization's warehouses were robbed: tons of cocaine and cash stashed in trucks, about ten million dollars. Sánchez claimed the heist. That is the money in the flyer.

The police attributed the killing to Carlos Mantilla Ceballos, alias Choclo, the longtime leader of Los Lagartos, who is blamed for the murder of Efraín Ruales. As the days passed, that became hard to believe: Marino was loyal to him, and some facts raise suspicion. Nearly a dozen conversations with people in the organization point to Sánchez, for two reasons. The heart: Marino suspected that Sánchez, in love, was telling Garcés things and that was how shipments were being lost. The wallet: killing the leader makes you the next one. Los Lagartos were born in Guayaquil's prisons as an alliance of hitmen and gangs to stop Los Choneros; they are 25 to 40 men who decide who lives in the Provisional Detention Center, export cocaine to Rotterdam with Serbian and Moroccan partners, and patented a method: locate enemy groups' warehouses, steal the merchandise and ship it themselves.

Sánchez could not do it alone. Marino's security circle included Edwin Egas Alemán, alias Mono, in charge of bribing customs and port officials; Fernando García Álava, alias Conejo, in charge of bringing in the merchandise, meaning drugs stolen from enemy groups; and Carlos García Álava, alias Carlitos, who ran the team of hitmen that murdered Samir on Marino's orders and later finished off Marino himself. Sánchez and one of the Garcías are former police officers; the squad mixes hitmen with ex-officers. Almost all of them were at the field, walked away unharmed and then called to claim they were the new bosses. An Instagram story by Micaela Morales puts them all at one table: Garcés, Wendy Landa, Morales, her brother, Eduardo Freire and Sánchez, in one of his few photos. All of them had denied knowing one another.

That day, Micaela Morales, Marino's fiancée, had her phone flooded with messages bearing a lizard emoji, the claim of responsibility for the murder. She got a call that, she told her inner circle, came from Pedro Sánchez: she should find Marino's watches and hand them over. Before changing her number, she asked for a list of his assets around the world, not just those in Dubai: several tens of millions. She told her lawyers she thought she had fallen in love with a businessman. She is out of the country, avoiding the courts. The organization's money moved through companies in the names of Alexandra Burgos, Marino's ex-wife, and Adriana Fuentes Pacho, Sánchez's former live-in partner, at a multi-store where Sánchez received money from the Chinese mafia and where the January 7 robbery may have happened. After the January report they dissolved the company, and on February 2, 2026, they created another in the name of Burgos's brother.

Installment 3

Kada's police officers

Carlos Kada was a drug trafficker. On his phone there was a photo of a photo, taken from an app that blocks screenshots, showing his hand and his fingerprints: four active-duty National Police officers posing with a seizure and sending it, not to their superiors, but to him, to let him know his competitors' drugs were already in police hands. Seizing drugs not to fight trafficking but to help a trafficker do better business. The report also includes photos of the vehicles intelligence agents use to tail traffickers and the confidential police reports Kada had in his possession.

Through the Anti-Corruption Unit, known as ULCO, Kada gave the police the names of officers in that same unit who worked for the organization of alias Gato Farfán, a drug trafficker far bigger than Fito, captured in Colombia and jailed in the United States as a DEA target. The report was received by police commander Fausto Salinas, who delegated it to General Pablo Ramírez, later jailed in the Metástasis case. Within hours the hunt for Kada began. According to sources, Gato Farfán found out and ordered alias Blanco, someone close to Kada, to kill him as an informant; nearly all the Kadas fell. In the photo, according to sources, the man taking the selfie is Major Luis Naula, of the National Unit for the Investigation of Transnational Crimes, the unit that works most closely with the United States embassy; to his right is Lieutenant Paul Aguilar, of the same unit. The police were investigating the other two, hampered by the image's low resolution. The report with the names is with the National Police.

The information isn't here on my channel, it's over there in your offices, General Commander.

Andersson Boscán, March 13, 2026
